## Sharp total variation from spherical rearrangement

[**P47: Sharp total variation bounds from spherical rearrangement**](papers/P47-sobolev-variation/README.md), by **Henry Zweiman**, now proves the complete variation identity and equality classification in derivative order three at every exponent, with the measure interpretation at p=1. The proof starts with an arbitrary maximizer, establishes regularity from a conserved residual quantity, extends the positive-action comparison to the endpoint, and determines the unique one-peak profile explicitly. Together with the finite-exponent theorem and measure endpoint, this completes the order-three case. Earlier results include the all-order Hilbert case, all-order height symmetry, and local exponent intervals for variation. Revision 1.13 proves all-order uniform-endpoint regularity: every maximizer saturates its highest derivative, has finitely many simple critical points, and has exactly n+r-1 simple residual zeros when there are r critical points. For each fixed n>=3, the critical-point count is uniformly bounded over all 1<p<=infinity. The bound is implicit; the remaining all-order variation conjecture is open. Revision 1.14 derives the endpoint switching Hessian with atomic root weights and polynomial projection. It also proves that V(n,infinity) is real algebraic in every order and admits a maximizing spline with algebraic knots and piece coefficients. The proof combines finite switching with a rational semialgebraic description and credited quantifier elimination. No effective stopping count or general higher-order multiple-peak exclusion is obtained. Revision 1.15 excludes every two-critical-point endpoint variation maximizer and every midpoint-symmetric three-critical-point endpoint maximizer for n>=3. It also proves that any remaining three-point candidate has its dilation-response minimum strictly outside the two outer critical points. The proof uses an atomic affine response, positive-density approximation, separated zeros and an order-four boundary sign count. These exclusions and the response condition now hold over every 1<p<=infinity; the general higher-order conjecture remains open.

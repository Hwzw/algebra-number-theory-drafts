# Problem and significance assessment

**Paper:** P36, *Dominance and common deformations of short local rings*, Henry Zweiman.

## Published question and known progress

[Kobayashi–Takahashi, arXiv:2603.10810v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.10810v1), Question 7.1, asks whether a Gorenstein local ring that is not a complete intersection must be dominant if its maximal ideal has cube zero or if its multiplicity is at most embedding codimension plus two. Section 7 of the author-hosted manuscript was checked, including the exact question and supporting partial results.

[Kimura, arXiv:2609.05781v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05781v1), posted September 5, 2026, proves the Artinian case for rings containing a field, and the higher-dimensional equicharacteristic case with infinite residue field (Theorem 7.3). The proof chain through Sections 2–4, especially Theorem 4.5, and the Section 7 application were read. This is the essential prior theorem, not a result of the present paper. The first Gorenstein dominant non-complete-intersection examples are already Kimura's.

## Proposed contribution

The common deformation applies to every Artinian local ring with maximal-ideal cube zero, without a Gorenstein hypothesis or a residue-field restriction. It presents the ring and its associated graded ring as quotients of a common complete Cohen–Macaulay ring by regular elements outside the maximal-ideal square. Dominance and uniform dominance therefore transfer in both directions, with dominant-index inequalities in the finite case.

Applied to the Gorenstein class, it removes the coefficient-field restriction in Kimura's theorem. The standard Artinian reduction, followed by [Liu's flat-descent theorem](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.14283v2) for finite residue fields, gives the full statement of Question 7.1. These are combined into one paper.

## Reusable mathematical content

The deformation transfers any property known to be equivalent across regular linear-parameter quotients. The paper proves the dominance and uniform-dominance applications explicitly. Its broader scope includes non-Gorenstein short rings, but their dominance is reduced to a graded problem, not automatically proved. A future dominant-index estimate for the graded Gorenstein class would immediately give one in unequal characteristic. This is a plausible route to further use; no citation-count prediction is justified.

## Priority audit and limits

Searches combined short and cube-zero local rings, Gorenstein rings, associated graded rings, common deformations, Cohen rings, mixed or unequal characteristic, and dominance. No matching characteristic-free dominance transfer or full Question 7.1 solution was located in the checked sources as of September 9, 2026. The recent Kimura source showed only version 1 on arXiv at the time of review.

Earlier work by Avramov–Iyengar–Şega on short rings and Rossi–Şega on compressed Gorenstein rings was screened for structural antecedents. Their known free-resolution and Poincaré-series results are not claimed here. This was not an exhaustive historical audit of all lifting constructions. A search without a matching result is evidence about the search, not a certificate of novelty.

The paper is an internally checked proposed preprint. Independent expert validation, historical priority, and significance remain provisional. It is not a proof that all short Gorenstein rings are uniformly dominant, nor a solution for rings of arbitrary Loewy length.

## Why this meets the intended depth

The sharp constant is a supremum over an infinite-dimensional class. A fixed-test boundary expansion proves only a lower bound. The upper bound must rule out concentration in an unbounded exterior, retain all nonlinear maximizing limits, and remain accurate when energies vanish arbitrarily quickly compared with temperature. Sections 3--7 address those three issues. The proof needs neither a unique nonlinear optimizer nor a nondegenerate Hessian of the variational quotient. Proposition 8.1 demonstrates that the nonlinear alternative is necessary within the smooth strictly convex setting, rather than on an excluded singular example.

The reusable result is the uniform entropy expansion under only energy control. It can support future perturbation arguments after their own tail hypotheses are verified. The theorem also explains why knowledge of the first eigenvalue alone does not generally determine the low-temperature logarithmic Sobolev derivative. These are substantive analytic contributions with a plausible audience in functional inequalities, singular perturbations and diffusion theory. They justify proceeding at the user's intended research depth; they do not predict acceptance by Advances in Mathematics or Transactions of the AMS.

## Scope and uncertainty

Convexity, a smooth bounded domain of minima, quadratic nondegenerate normal growth and a Laplacian growth bound are retained. Other exponents and degenerate boundary profiles remain open here. The coefficient is an implicit variational maximum; the paper does not calculate the limiting sharp constant or classify extremizers on a general domain. These restrictions limit breadth and should be explicit in any submission.

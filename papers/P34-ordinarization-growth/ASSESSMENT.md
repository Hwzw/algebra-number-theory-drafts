Theorem 6.1 establishes a growing-ordinarization limit on the scale r^3/g under r^2/g -> 0. It uses necessary multiplicity-chain conditions to bound every valid tuple outside the bulk and a hypergeometric collision argument inside the bulk. This goes beyond a pointwise fixed-r expansion and supplies an explicit transition law. The theorem and its proof, source comparison, and remaining limitations are reviewed in [uniform-review.md](uniform-review.md).

The result still does not settle either all-genus counting conjecture. It does not treat r proportional to g, does not give a relative exponential estimate at infinite r^3/g, and does not control errors at the scale needed to prove consecutive differences positive. The full publicly well-known conjecture remains unresolved, so the thread goal remains active. The publication is an internally audited, AI-assisted preprint revision and carries no independent correctness or exhaustive novelty certification.

## Explicit problem and primary evidence

Sogol Cyrusian and Nathan Kaplan, *Ordinarization numbers of numerical semigroups*, Communications in Algebra (2026), 1–24, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1080/00927872.2026.2615092), [arXiv v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.10222v2). The publisher records online publication on February 11, 2026. The arXiv version header is dated March 10, 2026; its HTML document date differs. The version identifier and retrieved bytes, rather than an inferred latest-edit date, identify the audited source.

The audit read the introduction, the fixed-ordinarization section including the proof of Theorem 3.4, the small-case formulas, and Remark 3.7. The latter explicitly asks whether the fixed-r quasipolynomial holds at all positive genera. The source's Conjecture 1.5 states the stronger all-genus monotonicity conjecture. Its r=1 and r=2 results precede this manuscript. The relevant source PDF pages 8–9 were also visually inspected.

Beck and Robins, *Computing the Continuous Discretely*, second edition, [publisher](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-1-4939-2969-6), supply standard rational Ehrhart theory. Theorem 3.23 was checked in the [author-hosted draft](https://matthbeck.github.io/papers/ccd.pdf). No third-party source PDF is redistributed here.

## Proposed advance

The manuscript answers Remark 3.7 for arbitrary fixed positive r by keeping strict inequalities homogeneous and including every mixed-sum closure constraint. It then determines the universal leading coefficient and the parity oscillation of the next coefficient. These yield eventual strict monotonicity for each fixed r, a partial result toward Conjecture 1.5.

Cyrusian and Kaplan already use rational polyhedra and, for r=2, a bulk with exceptional additive equalities. Those ideas are credited antecedents. The proposed additional argument works for arbitrary r and observes that the relevant exceptional hyperplanes pass through the origin, forcing their top-dimensional correction coefficient to be constant. This controls consecutive differences; an unstructured error estimate would not suffice.

## Significance and scope

The strongest reason for a distinct paper is the combination of an explicit published question with an all-r partial result on semigroup enumeration monotonicity. It provides a reusable separation between a full-dimensional counting region and exceptional additive loci. Potential further work includes full periods, the constant part of the next coefficient, effective thresholds, and all-genus injections.

This remains a focused contribution, not a resolution of total semigroup-count monotonicity or the full fixed-ordinarization conjecture. No citation-count forecast, independent correctness certification, journal acceptance, or confirmed historical priority is asserted. One consolidated manuscript is counted; its individual theorems are not separate papers.

# Internal proof review

Henry Zweiman. September 15, 2026.

This is the originating assistant's audit of its own argument, not independent peer review. No subagents or outside mathematicians were used in this stage.

1. **Spaces and normalization.** For n>=2, differentiation is an energy isometry from the clamped order-n space onto the zero-mean subspace of the clamped order-(n-1) space. The final endpoint condition on u is exactly the integral constraint on u'. Real scalars are specified.
2. **Jacobi identity.** In the 2m integrations by parts, the derivative orders in each boundary product sum to 2m-1, so at least one is below m and vanishes. The leading coefficient gives (k+2m)!/k!, with the sign canceled by the leading coefficient of x^m(1-x)^m. Energy orthogonality and polynomial density give a complete basis, not just a formal expansion.
3. **Constraint and sphere dimension.** The mean representer is w/(2m)!. Deleting its mode yields Q_m with coefficients M/lambda_k. On S^(2m+2), the affine coordinate density is proportional to x^m(1-x)^m. The normalized zonal harmonic basis matches the Jacobi basis without an additional multiplicity factor.
4. **Poisson mixture.** The beta integral equals k!/(k+2m)!. Tonelli applies to nonnegative spectral terms. Subtracting the constant harmonic is essential and is preserved at fixed beta-weighted measure. No interchange of a conditionally convergent Green kernel series is needed.
5. **Rearrangement and equality.** Burchard-Hajaiej Theorem 2 applies to two identical indicators, strictly supermodular product integrand, and positive bounded strictly distance-decreasing Poisson kernels. Integrated equality forces equality at some radius. Zonal invariance and the nonzero cap centroid fix the cap axis up to reversal. Null sets for beta measure and Lebesgue measure coincide in the open interval.
6. **Duality and optimizer.** Layer cake represents any load in [-1,1] as an average of signs; convexity justifies restricting the supremum. Constants are annihilated, giving the factor four. An endpoint interval represents point evaluation after integration. The known point diagonal has its unique maximum at 1/2. For an actual extremizer, the sign of u' has both signs on positive-measure sets. The equality chain and Riesz uniqueness then give the full classification.
7. **Moment geometry.** Norm below one excludes extremality by scaling; zero is excluded separately. n+2 positive-measure pieces give a nontrivial perturbation satisfying n moments and one variation constraint. Vandermonde excludes <=n nodes. Only necessity of the n+1-node description is used. Divided-difference weights and the n! factor agree with the simplex density normalization.
8. **Endpoint compactness.** The potential map is weak-star to uniform continuous for n>=2. Variation is a supremum of continuous linear functionals, since the integrated bounded load kernel H_g is continuous, including n=2. The closed convex variation sublevel set contains the extreme points, so Krein-Milman proves the bound. Height compactness supplies attainment; upper semicontinuity of variation is not assumed.
9. **Endpoint coincidence.** Extreme points of the exposed maximizing face are extreme in the original moment ball. Scaling a shorter support to the full interval increases height by L^(1-n) at fixed derivative-measure norm. All resulting full-support splines share orientation by their initial derivative signs. Root concavity gives strictly signed derivatives outside the maximum interval; disjoint maximum intervals contradict the common norming load. Pairwise intersecting closed intervals have a common point, and weak-star continuity of evaluation extends this point to the face.
10. **Recovery.** Mollification of the zero extension and compression with amplitude (1+2 epsilon)^(1-n) preserve the bound on the nth derivative's L1 norm. Smooth endpoint jets vanish, while the height and variation converge. This proves equality of ordinary and relaxed constants, without asserting ordinary attainment.

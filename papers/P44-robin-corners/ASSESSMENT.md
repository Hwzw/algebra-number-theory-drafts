# Priority and significance assessment

Henry Zweiman. September 15, 2026. P44, developed as Q52.

## Internal decision

The completed argument supports publication as one substantial research preprint. The originating assistant's proof audit found no remaining gap in the stated polygonal theorem. This is an internal research judgment, not independent expert review, an absolute priority certificate, or a prediction of journal acceptance.

The theorem says that, for every bounded convex polygon that is neither tangential nor rectangular, the positive parameters at which the Robin ground state is log-concave form a locally finite set. It therefore provides fixed-domain counterexamples to the eventual log-concavity conjecture, with arbitrarily large bad parameters. The stronger local statement excludes semiconcavity of both the eigenfunction and its logarithm outside an analytic zero set. A parallelogram and its products are examples within this one contribution.

The final statement was read in the publisher PDF through the web tool. There is no smoothness qualification in that statement. ## Substantive step

Analytic dependence in H1 alone does not imply analytic dependence of a corner asymptotic coefficient. Nor is C2 a closed condition in that space. The proof therefore changes the Robin boundary condition locally to a fixed Neumann condition, obtains analytic H2 dependence through a fixed graph space, and expresses the coefficient as an explicit weighted integral plus an arc trace. The integral is continuous in an Lp space available for the forcing even though the eigenfunction itself does not have the corresponding W2,p regularity.

Semiconcavity would then force a bounded Hessian through the differential equation. This makes the extracted coefficient vanish. Its first derivative at the Neumann parameter is nonzero by the prior classification, so the analytic identity theorem prevents an interval of concave parameters anywhere on the positive axis. The maximum-principle proof of corner positivity avoids an unverified boundary Harnack assumption.

This resolves a published conjecture in its stated generality, and identifies a broad class of domains on which failure persists at arbitrarily large parameters. The mechanism may apply to other analytic families of corner problems whose desired geometric property forces stronger regularity. These are substantive reasons to seek expert spectral-analysis/PDE review at the program's intended standard. The proof is short and draws on substantial existing corner theory; a journal may assess its breadth or novelty differently. Advances in Mathematics or Transactions of the AMS suitability is not certified here.

## Boundaries and counting

Count at most one paper after exact public-file verification. Do not count the parallelogram, dimensional products, semiconcavity strengthening, or individual lemmas separately. The exceptional set need not be empty or globally finite. No single smooth counterexample to eventual concavity is constructed. Tangential polygons, higher-dimensional quasiconcavity, and the sharp fundamental gap remain outside the theorem. Independent mathematical review is still needed.

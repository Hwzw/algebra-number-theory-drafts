# Irreducible compositions in an exceptional unicritical family

Henry Zweiman

## Abstract

We construct irreducible compositions in a positive-shift subfamily left exceptional by a recent theorem on unicritically generated semigroups. Let $`d\geq4`$ be even and let $`a\geq2`$ be an integer which is not a $`p`$th power for any odd prime $`p\mid d`$, nor a fourth power when $`4\mid d`$. Put $`f(x)=x^d+a-a^d`$ and $`g(x)=x^d+a`$. We prove that $`f\circ g\circ g\circ h`$ is irreducible over $`\mathbb{Q}`$ for every composition $`h`$ of integral unicritical polynomials whose degrees have prime divisors among those of $`d`$. For a finite equal-degree generating set containing $`f`$ and $`g`$, this gives an explicit positive lower density. Within the exceptional four-constant alphabet, the shorter prefix $`f\circ g`$ suffices. The result includes infinitely many pairs in every degree divisible by four for which $`g`$ is reducible and $`f`$ fixes a square. The proof combines one real embedding, a field norm, and gaps between consecutive prime powers.

## 1. Introduction and statements

Let $`\mathcal A`$ be a finite set of polynomials of degree at least two in $`\mathbb{Z}[x]`$, and let $`G=\langle\mathcal A\rangle`$ be the semigroup they generate under composition. A natural question is whether the presence of one irreducible polynomial forces a positive proportion of the elements of $`G`$ to be irreducible over $`\mathbb{Q}`$, when polynomials are counted by degree. For generators of common degree $`d`$, write
```math
\underline\delta(G)=\liminf_{B\to\infty}
 \frac{\#\{F\in G:\deg F\leq B,\ F\text{ irreducible over }\mathbb{Q}\}}
 {\#\{F\in G:\deg F\leq B\}}.
```
The prime-degree case was treated in [\[2\]](#ref-HJK), following the quadratic work [\[3\]](#ref-HJY). For general common degree, Bhardwaj, Boyer-Paulet, Hindes, Qiu, and Sun [\[1, Theorem 1.1\]](#ref-BBHQS) establish the desired positive-proportion conclusion outside explicit exceptional families. In even degree, the exceptional constants are contained in

<a id="label-eq-exception"></a>

```math
\tag{1}
 \{y^p-y^{pd},\ y^p,\ -y^p,\ -y^p-y^{pd}\},
 \qquad p\mid d\text{ prime},\quad y\in\mathbb{Z}.
```

They expect the positive-proportion conclusion to extend to the exceptional cases. We treat a positive-shift subfamily of [(1)](#label-eq-exception).

Throughout, superscripts on integers denote ordinary powers. We write compositions explicitly, including $`g\circ g`$ rather than $`g^2`$, to distinguish iteration from multiplication. The empty composition is the identity polynomial.

<a id="theorem-1"></a>

**Theorem 1.1.**

<a id="label-thm-main"></a>

Let $`d\geq4`$ be even, and let $`a\geq2`$ be an integer satisfying

1.  $`a\notin\mathbb{Z}^p`$ for every odd prime $`p\mid d`$;

2.  if $`4\mid d`$, then $`a\notin\mathbb{Z}^4`$.

Define
```math
D=a^d-a,\qquad f(x)=x^d-D,\qquad g(x)=x^d+a,
 \qquad P=f\circ g\circ g.
```
Then $`P\circ h`$ is irreducible over $`\mathbb{Q}`$ for every finite composition $`h`$ of polynomials $`x^m+c`$ with $`c\in\mathbb{Z}`$, $`m\geq2`$, and $`\operatorname{rad}(m)\mid\operatorname{rad}(d)`$. This includes the empty composition.

<!-- end theorem-1 -->

<a id="corollary-1"></a>

**Corollary 1.2.**

<a id="label-cor-density"></a>

Under the hypotheses of Theorem [1.1](#label-thm-main), let $`G`$ be generated by $`r`$ distinct polynomials $`x^d+c_i`$, with $`c_i\in\mathbb{Z}`$, including $`f`$ and $`g`$. Then
```math
\underline\delta(G)\geq r^{-3}.
```
If instead the generating set is a subset of

<a id="label-eq-alphabet"></a>

```math
\tag{2}
 \{x^d+a-a^d,\ x^d+a,\ x^d-a,\ x^d-a-a^d\}
```

containing $`f`$ and $`g`$, then $`f\circ g\circ h`$ is irreducible for every $`h\in G`$ and for the empty composition, and
```math
\underline\delta(G)\geq r^{-2}.
```
In particular, $`\underline\delta(\langle f,g\rangle)\geq1/4`$.

<!-- end corollary-1 -->

The main new instances occur when $`4\mid d`$ and $`a=4z^4`$. If $`z`$ is odd and nonzero, the hypotheses hold for every such $`d`$: the valuation $`v_2(a)=2`$ excludes every odd perfect power and every fourth power. For $`d=2^k`$, $`k\geq2`$, every nonzero integer $`z`$ is allowed. In these instances $`a=(2z^2)^2`$ and $`f(a)=a`$, while

<a id="label-eq-sophie"></a>

```math
\tag{3}
 g(x)=\bigl(x^{d/2}-2zx^{d/4}+2z^2\bigr)
       \bigl(x^{d/2}+2zx^{d/4}+2z^2\bigr)
```

is reducible. Thus an irreducible positive-shift generator is not available to obtain the conclusion by the existing norm argument. The first example is
```math
f=x^4-252,\qquad g=x^4+4.
```
The two-letter prefix $`f\circ g`$ gives the lower bound $`1/4`$ in the semigroup generated by this pair.

The argument in [\[1, Proposition 3.8\]](#ref-BBHQS) excludes the positive-shift partner in [(2)](#label-eq-alphabet). Its norm sufficient condition [\[1, Proposition 3.1\]](#ref-BBHQS) gives no conclusion at the first transition here, since $`f(g(0))=a`$ is a square in the displayed subfamily. Our proof deals with that transition using a negative real root of $`f`$ and then separates the fourth-power obstruction by its norm. All later transitions follow from elementary gaps between prime powers. We do not resolve the full exceptional locus [(1)](#label-eq-exception).

## 2. Algebraic irreducibility criteria

We use the classical binomial criterion: for a field $`K`$ of characteristic zero and $`n\geq2`$, the polynomial $`x^n-b`$ is irreducible over $`K`$ if and only if

<a id="label-eq-binomial"></a>

```math
\tag{4}
 b\notin K^p\quad(p\mid n\text{ prime}),\qquad
 b\notin-4K^4\quad\text{if }4\mid n.
```

See [\[4, Chapter VI, Section 9, Theorem 9.1\]](#ref-Lang). The exceptional fourth-power clause in [(4)](#label-eq-binomial) is essential in composite even degree.

<a id="lemma-1"></a>

**Lemma 2.1 (Capelli’s composition criterion).**

<a id="label-lem-capelli"></a>

Let $`A\in K[x]`$ be irreducible, let $`\alpha`$ be a root of $`A`$, and let $`B\in K[x]`$ be nonconstant. Then $`A\circ B`$ is irreducible over $`K`$ if and only if $`B(x)-\alpha`$ is irreducible over $`K(\alpha)`$.

<!-- end lemma-1 -->

<a id="proof-1"></a>

**Proof.**

If $`\beta`$ is a root of $`B(x)-\alpha`$, then $`\alpha=B(\beta)`$ and $`K(\alpha)\subseteq K(\beta)`$. The tower law gives
```math
[K(\beta):K]=[K(\beta):K(\alpha)]\deg A.
```
The right side equals $`(\deg B)(\deg A)`$ precisely when $`B(x)-\alpha`$ is irreducible over $`K(\alpha)`$. Since $`\beta`$ is a root of $`A\circ B`$, the equality is equivalent to irreducibility of that polynomial. $`\square`$

<!-- end proof-1 -->

The following familiar norm consequence is included to specify the parity and fourth-power issue exactly; compare [\[1, Proposition 3.1\]](#ref-BBHQS).

<a id="lemma-2"></a>

**Lemma 2.2.**

<a id="label-lem-norm"></a>

Let $`A\in\mathbb{Q}[x]`$ be monic and irreducible of even degree $`N`$, and let $`B=x^m+c`$ with $`m\geq2`$ and $`c\in\mathbb{Q}`$. If $`A(c)\notin\mathbb{Q}^p`$ for every prime $`p\mid m`$, then $`A\circ B`$ is irreducible over $`\mathbb{Q}`$.

<!-- end lemma-2 -->

<a id="proof-2"></a>

**Proof.**

Suppose otherwise, and put $`K=\mathbb{Q}(\alpha)`$ for a root $`\alpha`$ of $`A`$. Lemma [2.1](#label-lem-capelli) and [(4)](#label-eq-binomial) imply either $`\alpha-c=u^p`$ for a prime $`p\mid m`$, or $`4\mid m`$ and $`\alpha-c=-4u^4`$, with $`u\in K`$. In the first case its norm is a rational $`p`$th power. In the second case its norm is
```math
(-4)^N\mathrm{N}_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(u)^4,
```
which is a rational square because $`N`$ is even. In both cases this contradicts the assumptions, since
```math
\mathrm{N}_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(\alpha-c)=(-1)^N A(c)=A(c).
```
$`\square`$

<!-- end proof-2 -->

## 3. The first mixed composition

<a id="lemma-3"></a>

**Lemma 3.1.**

<a id="label-lem-gap"></a>

Let $`d\geq4`$ be even and $`p\mid d`$ be prime. If $`b\geq2`$ is an integer, then
```math
(b^{d/p})^p-(b^{d/p}-1)^p>b^{d-d/p}\geq b^2.
```

<!-- end lemma-3 -->

<a id="proof-3"></a>

**Proof.**

For an integer $`u\geq2`$, factor the difference of powers to obtain
```math
u^p-(u-1)^p=\sum_{j=0}^{p-1}u^{p-1-j}(u-1)^j>u^{p-1}.
```
Apply this with $`u=b^{d/p}`$. Since $`d\geq4`$ is even, $`d-d/p\geq2`$. $`\square`$

<!-- end proof-3 -->

<a id="proposition-1"></a>

**Proposition 3.2.**

<a id="label-prop-first"></a>

Under the hypotheses of Theorem [1.1](#label-thm-main), both $`f`$ and $`f\circ g`$ are irreducible over $`\mathbb{Q}`$.

<!-- end proposition-1 -->

<a id="proof-4"></a>

**Proof.**

For every prime $`p\mid d`$, Lemma [3.1](#label-lem-gap) gives
```math
(a^{d/p}-1)^p<a^d-a=D<(a^{d/p})^p.
```
Thus $`D\notin\mathbb{Z}^p`$, hence $`D\notin\mathbb{Q}^p`$. Here we use the elementary fact that an integer which is a rational $`p`$th power is an integer $`p`$th power. Since $`D>0`$, it also does not belong to $`-4\mathbb{Q}^4`$. Criterion [(4)](#label-eq-binomial) proves $`f`$ irreducible.

Choose its negative real root $`\alpha=-D^{1/d}`$ and regard $`K=\mathbb{Q}(\alpha)`$ as a subfield of $`\mathbb R`$. The element $`\alpha-a`$ is negative in this embedding, so it is not a square in $`K`$. Also

<a id="label-eq-norma"></a>

```math
\tag{5}
 \mathrm{N}_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(\alpha-a)=f(a)=a.
```

For an odd prime $`p\mid d`$, equation [(5)](#label-eq-norma) and the hypothesis $`a\notin\mathbb{Z}^p`$ exclude $`\alpha-a\in K^p`$.

It remains to address the exceptional clause when $`4\mid d`$. If $`\alpha-a=-4u^4`$ for some $`u\in K`$, then, since $`[K:\mathbb{Q}]=d`$,
```math
a=(-4)^d\mathrm{N}_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(u)^4
   =\bigl(4^{d/4}\mathrm{N}_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(u)\bigr)^4.
```
This would make $`a`$ a rational fourth power, and therefore an integer fourth power, contrary to hypothesis. Thus [(4)](#label-eq-binomial) proves $`g(x)-\alpha=x^d-(\alpha-a)`$ irreducible over $`K`$. Lemma [2.1](#label-lem-capelli) proves $`f\circ g`$ irreducible over $`\mathbb{Q}`$. $`\square`$

<!-- end proof-4 -->

## 4. Uniform propagation

<a id="proof-5"></a>

**Proof of Theorem [1.1](#label-thm-main).**

If $`y\geq a^d+a`$ is an integer, then $`D<y`$. For every prime $`p\mid d`$, Lemma [3.1](#label-lem-gap) therefore gives

<a id="label-eq-valuegap"></a>

```math
\tag{6}
 (y^{d/p}-1)^p<f(y)=y^d-D<(y^{d/p})^p.
```

In particular, $`f(y)`$ is not a rational $`p`$th power for any prime $`p\mid d`$.

Put $`H=f\circ g`$, irreducible by Proposition [3.2](#label-prop-first). Since $`g(a)=a^d+a`$, equation [(6)](#label-eq-valuegap) applies to $`H(a)`$. Lemma [2.2](#label-lem-norm) now proves $`P=H\circ g`$ irreducible.

For every $`t\in\mathbb{Z}`$, evenness of $`d`$ implies $`g(t)=t^d+a\geq a`$, and hence
```math
g(g(t))\geq a^d+a.
```
Equation [(6)](#label-eq-valuegap) yields

<a id="label-eq-allvalues"></a>

```math
\tag{7}
 P(t)\notin\mathbb{Q}^p\qquad(t\in\mathbb{Z},\ p\mid d\text{ prime}).
```

We can therefore append the factors of $`h`$ one at a time. Suppose that $`Q=P\circ h_0`$ is already irreducible, and append $`B=x^m+c`$ as in the theorem. Then $`h_0(c)\in\mathbb{Z}`$, so
```math
Q(c)=P(h_0(c))\notin\mathbb{Q}^p\qquad(p\mid m\text{ prime})
```
by [(7)](#label-eq-allvalues). The polynomial $`Q`$ remains monic and has even degree. Lemma [2.2](#label-lem-norm) proves $`Q\circ B`$ irreducible and completes the induction. $`\square`$

<!-- end proof-5 -->

<a id="proposition-2"></a>

**Proposition 4.1.**

<a id="label-prop-short"></a>

Under the hypotheses of Corollary [1.2](#label-cor-density) for the alphabet [(2)](#label-eq-alphabet), $`f\circ g\circ h`$ is irreducible for every finite word $`h`$ in that alphabet, including the empty word.

<!-- end proposition-2 -->

<a id="proof-6"></a>

**Proof.**

Put $`T=\{t\in\mathbb{Z}:|t|\geq a\}`$. All four generator constants belong to $`T`$. The first three maps in [(2)](#label-eq-alphabet) take $`T`$ into integers at least $`a`$, because $`a^d-a\geq a`$. For the last map, an input of magnitude $`a`$ maps to $`-a`$. For all other inputs in $`T`$, integrality gives $`|t|\geq a+1`$, and
```math
(a+1)^d-a^d\geq d a^{d-1}>2a.
```
Thus its output is greater than $`a`$. Consequently every word in the alphabet preserves $`T`$.

For $`t\in T`$ we have $`g(t)\geq a^d+a`$, so [(6)](#label-eq-valuegap) implies that $`H(t)=(f\circ g)(t)`$ is not a rational $`p`$th power for any prime $`p\mid d`$. Starting from $`H`$ irreducible by Proposition [3.2](#label-prop-first), append letters as before. Each required norm value has the form $`H(h_0(c))`$, where the generator constant $`c`$ belongs to $`T`$ and $`h_0(c)\in T`$. Lemma [2.2](#label-lem-norm) proves irreducibility at every step. $`\square`$

<!-- end proof-6 -->

## 5. Counting by degree

We include the elementary same-degree freeness argument to pass from words to actual polynomials; compare [\[1, Proposition 3.9\]](#ref-BBHQS).

<a id="lemma-4"></a>

**Lemma 5.1.**

<a id="label-lem-free"></a>

In characteristic zero, distinct polynomials $`x^d+c_1,\ldots,x^d+c_r`$, with common degree $`d\geq2`$, generate a free semigroup under composition.

<!-- end lemma-4 -->

<a id="proof-7"></a>

**Proof.**

Degree determines word length. Suppose two words of equal length greater than one define the same polynomial. Removing their outer letters leaves monic polynomials $`U,V`$ of equal positive degree $`M`$, with
```math
U^d+b=V^d+c.
```
If $`U\ne V`$, then
```math
\deg(U^d-V^d)=(d-1)M+\deg(U-V)>0,
```
since $`(U^d-V^d)/(U-V)`$ has leading coefficient $`d`$ and degree $`(d-1)M`$. This contradicts $`U^d-V^d=c-b`$. Hence $`U=V`$ and $`b=c`$. Induction recovers all letters. $`\square`$

<!-- end proof-7 -->

<a id="proof-8"></a>

**Proof of Corollary [1.2](#label-cor-density).**

By Lemma [5.1](#label-lem-free), there are exactly $`r^n`$ distinct polynomials of word length $`n`$, each of degree $`d^n`$. Every word of length $`n\geq3`$ with prefix $`fgg`$ is irreducible by Theorem [1.1](#label-thm-main); there are $`r^{n-3}`$ such words. Therefore, for $`N=\lfloor\log_d B\rfloor\geq3`$,
```math
\frac{\#\{F\in G:\deg F\leq B,\ F\text{ irreducible}\}}
 {\#\{F\in G:\deg F\leq B\}}
 \geq\frac{\sum_{n=3}^{N}r^{n-3}}{\sum_{n=1}^{N}r^n}.
```
Since $`f\ne g`$, we have $`r\geq2`$, and the right side tends to $`r^{-3}`$. For the restricted alphabet, Proposition [4.1](#label-prop-short) supplies the prefix $`fg`$ of length two, giving the analogous limit $`r^{-2}`$. $`\square`$

<!-- end proof-8 -->

<a id="remark-1"></a>

**Remark 5.2.**

The restrictions on $`a`$ in Theorem [1.1](#label-thm-main) are sufficient hypotheses for the real-place and norm proof; no necessity assertion is intended. The result does not settle exceptional pairs with the negative shift $`x^d-a`$ in place of $`g`$, when the positive-shift generator is absent. The mixed-degree assertion concerns irreducibility of the indicated compositions, not a density theorem for a mixed-degree semigroup.

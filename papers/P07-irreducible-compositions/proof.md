# A real-place and norm argument for an exceptional dynamical family

2026-09-08. **Proof independently reviewed in full, including the even-degree extension and the restricted-alphabet corollary. Manuscript transfer also passed; see the portfolio review.**

## Main statement

Let d≥4 be an even integer. Let a≥2 be an integer which is not a p-th power for any odd prime p dividing d; if 4 divides d, assume also that a is not a fourth power. Put

`D=a^d−a`, `f(x)=x^d−D`, `g(x)=x^d+a`, and `P=f∘g∘g`.

**Theorem.** The polynomial P is irreducible over Q. More generally, P∘h is irreducible over Q for every finite composition h of polynomials `x^m+c`, where m≥2 has all its prime divisors among those of d, and c∈Z. The exponent m and constant c may vary from factor to factor. The empty composition h=x is permitted.

**Density corollary.** If G is generated by r distinct polynomials `x^d+c_i` with c_i∈Z and contains f and g among those generators, then

`liminf_{B→∞} #{irreducible F∈G : deg F≤B}/#{F∈G : deg F≤B} ≥ r^(−3)`.

In particular this applies to every `a=4z^4`, z∈Z\{0}, when d is a power of two at least four, and to every odd nonzero z when d is any positive multiple of four. In this subfamily g is reducible, whereas f has the square fixed point a; this is a family specifically left within the exception of Bhardwaj–Boyer-Paulet–Hindes–Qiu–Sun, arXiv:2510.10310, Theorem 1.1. Their exceptional locus is not asserted to consist of counterexamples. The theorem here resolves this subfamily, not their full exceptional locus.

## Classical algebra used

We use two standard forms of Capelli's criterion, in characteristic zero:

1. If A∈K[x] is irreducible and α is one of its roots, then A∘B is irreducible over K if and only if B(x)−α is irreducible over K(α).
2. The binomial `x^n−b` is irreducible over K if and only if b is not a p-th power in K for every prime p dividing n, and, when 4 divides n, b is not of the form `−4v^4`, v∈K.

The second statement is understood for b≠0; zero is already a p-th power. For n a power of two, the only ordinary power obstruction is a square. These are the same standard criteria used in Proposition 3.1 of the source paper, which cites Lang, *Algebra*, 3rd ed., Theorem 9.1, p. 297 for the binomial criterion.

**Norm consequence.** Let A∈Q[x] be monic, irreducible, and of even degree N. Let B=x^m+c, m≥2, c∈Q. If A(c) is not a rational p-th power for any prime p dividing m, then A∘B is irreducible over Q.

*Proof.* Otherwise, for a root α of A and K=Q(α), the two criteria imply that α−c is either a p-th power for a prime p dividing m, or `−4v^4`, the latter only when 4 divides m. In the first case its norm is a p-th power. In the second case its norm is `(-4)^N Norm(v)^4`, a square since N is even and 2 divides m. But

`Norm(α−c)=(-1)^N A(c)=A(c)`,

a contradiction. □

## Step 1: irreducibility of f

For every prime p dividing d, set b=a^{d/p}. Since d is even and at least four, d−d/p≥2. The factorization of a difference of powers gives

`b^p−(b−1)^p = Σ_{j=0}^{p−1} b^(p−1−j)(b−1)^j > b^(p−1) = a^(d−d/p) ≥ a^2 > a`.

Consequently `(b−1)^p < D=a^d−a < b^p`. Thus D is not a p-th power in Z, hence not in Q, for every prime p dividing d. Since D is positive, it cannot equal `−4v^4` for v∈Q. The binomial criterion proves f irreducible.

## Step 2: crossing the square-norm obstruction

We prove that H=f∘g is irreducible. Choose the negative real root `α=−D^(1/d)` of f, and put K=Q(α), regarded as a subfield of R. We have

`g(x)−α=x^d−(α−a)`.

Since α−a<0 in this real field, it is not a square in K. Also `[K:Q]=d` is even, so `Norm(α−a)=f(a)=a`. If α−a were a p-th power in K for an odd prime p dividing d, taking norms would make a a rational p-th power, hence an integer p-th power, contrary to hypothesis. The remaining possible obstruction occurs only if 4 divides d, and would be

`α−a=−4v^4` for some v∈K.

However, `[K:Q]=d` and d is even, so

`Norm(α−a)=f(a)=a^d−D=a`.

Taking norms in the proposed obstruction gives

`a=(-4)^d Norm(v)^4=4^d Norm(v)^4=(4^(d/4) Norm(v))^4`.

Because 4 divides d, this would make a a fourth power in Q. An integer which is a fourth power in Q is a fourth power in Z, by writing the rational fourth root in lowest terms. This contradicts the hypothesis on a. Thus neither obstruction occurs, and the binomial criterion followed by Capelli proves H irreducible.

This step is the contribution beyond the direct norm-only argument: f(a)=a is a square when a=4z^4, so the usual square-norm sufficient condition provides no information. The selected real embedding rules out squares before the finer fourth-power norm is used.

## Step 3: a polynomial avoiding the relevant prime powers at integers

For any integer y≥a^d+a and any prime p dividing d, set b_y=y^{d/p}. The same difference-of-powers calculation gives

`b_y^p−(b_y−1)^p > y^(d−d/p) ≥ y^2 > D`.

It follows that `(b_y−1)^p < y^d−D=f(y) < b_y^p`. Thus f(y) is a positive integer and not a rational p-th power for any prime p dividing d.

Since `g(a)=a^d+a`, this shows that `H(a)=f(g(a))` avoids all those powers. H is monic, irreducible, and of even degree. The norm consequence proves `H∘g=P` irreducible.

Moreover, for every integer t, evenness of d gives `g(t)=t^d+a≥a`, whence `g(g(t))≥a^d+a`. The same inequality proves that **P(t) is not a rational p-th power for any prime p dividing d, for every t∈Z**.

## Step 4: arbitrary further composition

Proceed by induction on the length of h. The empty-composition case is Step 3. Suppose `Q=P∘h` is monic and irreducible, and append `B=x^m+c`, with m≥2 supported on the primes dividing d and c∈Z. Since every factor of h has integer coefficients, h(c)∈Z. Hence

`Q(c)=P(h(c))`

is not a rational p-th power for every prime p dividing m by Step 3. The degree of Q is even. Applying the norm consequence proves Q∘B irreducible. This completes the theorem. □

## Step 5: counting actual polynomials

For completeness, distinct words in distinct maps `x^d+c_i` give distinct polynomials. Indeed, degrees determine word length. If words of the same length greater than one give the same polynomial, write their outermost step as

`U(x)^d+b=V(x)^d+c`,

where U,V are monic of the same positive degree M. If U≠V, put q=deg(U−V)≥0. Characteristic zero and monicity imply

`deg(U^d−V^d)=(d−1)M+q>0`,

because `(U^d−V^d)/(U−V)` has leading coefficient d and degree (d−1)M. This cannot equal the constant c−b. Thus U=V and b=c. Induction recovers every letter. This also supplies a direct proof of the needed same-degree freeness without invoking Mason's theorem.

All words of length n≥3 beginning with the three-letter prefix fgg are irreducible by the theorem. There are exactly r^{n−3} such words among r^n words of length n. For N=floor(log_d B) and N≥3, the irreducible numerator is at least

`Σ_{n=3}^N r^{n−3}`,

and the denominator is `Σ_{n=1}^N r^n`. Since f and g are distinct, r≥2. Their quotient tends to r^(−3), proving the corollary. □

## A shorter prefix for the exceptional alphabet

**Corollary.** Under the theorem's hypotheses on d and a, suppose G is generated by an r-element subset of

`{x^d+a−a^d, x^d+a, x^d−a, x^d−a−a^d}`

containing f and g. Then H=f∘g, without the second g, satisfies H∘h irreducible for every h∈G, as well as the empty h. The lower density is at least r^(−2); in particular it is at least 1/4 for the pair {f,g}.

*Proof.* Put T={t∈Z:|t|≥a}. All four constants belong to T. All four maps preserve T. The first three take T into integers at least a, since a^d−a≥a. For the last map, inputs of magnitude a map to −a. Otherwise integrality gives |t|≥a+1, and

`(a+1)^d−a^d ≥ d a^(d−1) > 2a`,

so its output is greater than a. Thus every composed word sends T into T.

For t∈T, `g(t)≥a^d+a`, so the prime-power gap from Step3 shows that H(t)=f(g(t)) is not a rational p-th power for any prime p dividing d. Start from H irreducible by Step2 and append letters. At each step the relevant norm value is H(h(c)), with c a generator constant in T and h(c)∈T. The norm consequence proves irreducibility. Counting words with the length-two prefix fg exactly as in Step5 gives r^(−2). □

## Scope, not yet proved

- The theorem does not settle exceptional pairs containing `x^d−a` in place of g and no positive-shift generator g.
- It does not claim the full mixed-degree density problem, although its irreducibility statement permits mixed degrees with the specified prime support in the tail.
- It does not claim novelty from search absence. A theorem-level comparison with current papers and independent mathematical review are still required before any manuscript decision.
- No finite search is needed for the proof. Exact low-degree checks are sanity checks only.

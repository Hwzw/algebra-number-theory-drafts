## P14: scalar power-subsequence classification

[P14](papers/P14-scalar-power-repair/manuscript.md) gives a finite iff criterion for scalar recurrence power subsequences, including degeneracy and transients; an exact coefficient-field exponent in the nondegenerate case; an ultimately periodic exponent spectrum; and a terminating least-multiplier computation for s>1. It concerns Dold divisibility, not positivity or journal certification. The [assessment](papers/P14-scalar-power-repair/ASSESSMENT.md) records the original Minton PDF access limitation and provisional priority.

## P40: low-temperature logarithmic Sobolev boundary selection

The claimed theorem gives the sharp first-order coefficient for convex quadratic confinement around smooth bounded convex domains in every finite dimension, incorporating nonlinear extremizers and spectral limits. The unbounded exterior entropy estimate and its vanishing-energy uniformity are the principal proposed advances. Convexity, nondegenerate quadratic growth and a Laplacian bound remain hypotheses. The known spectral formula and interval asymptotic are credited; other boundary exponents and general extremizer classification remain open. See [the full assessment](papers/P40-log-sobolev-boundary-selection/ASSESSMENT.md).

## P41: uniform reverse Robin Kohler-Jobin inequality

The claimed theorem answers the planar case of Buttazzo--Cito--Solombrino Q2 for all bounded open Lipschitz sets, with a common exponent for every positive Robin parameter and area. The Robin p-Laplacian extension is restricted to 1<p<=n/(n-1), where a prior pointwise Talenti theorem applies. The torsion-composition boundary correction, linear spectral control and uniform exponent are the proposed advances. Faber--Krahn, its rigidity and quantitative Saint-Venant are credited inputs. Higher-dimensional linear Robin Q2, the least exponent and forward Q1 remain unresolved here. See [the assessment](papers/P41-robin-reverse-kohler-jobin/ASSESSMENT.md).

## P42: Euclidean persistence for anisotropic eigenvalue-torsion products

The claimed theorem identifies the Euclidean norm as the unique optimizer on every fixed ball, for sufficiently small positive product exponents in the maximum problem and sufficiently large ones in the minimum problem. It covers all normalized symmetric seminorms, every n>=2 and p>1. The two-sided angular-deficit estimate and optimal uniform-distance power (n+1)/2 belong to this single contribution. The known quadratic-seminorm ball theorem, Euclidean eigenvalue endpoint and convex symmetrization are credited. General domains, asymmetric anisotropies, optimal q thresholds and endpoint-uniform p estimates remain unresolved here. See [the assessment](papers/P42-anisotropic-persistence/ASSESSMENT.md).

## P43: genus obstruction for commuting boundary operators

The claimed theorem excludes every positive genus for compact connected oriented smooth surfaces with nonempty boundary and exactly commuting Dirichlet-to-Neumann map and boundary Laplacian. It addresses both cases of Speciel's final Open Problem 1.6; the full classification corollary uses his existing genus-zero theorem. Finite-period Hilbert-transform theory and smooth conformal sewing are credited. The new argument combines Fourier-invariant holomorphic traces with degree one and a capping Schur complement. Quantitative stability, nonorientable surfaces and rough boundaries are outside the result. See [the assessment](papers/P43-commuting-genus/ASSESSMENT.md).

## P44: convex Robin domains with vanishing spectral gaps

Revision 4.1 adds a complete elementary perimeter-scaled first-eigenvalue comparison in the plane. Area-one thin triangles have positive-parameter limit alpha/2, beating the disk for 0 < alpha <= 4 pi, and give unboundedness below for every negative alpha. The alpha=1 triangle witness and disk lower bound are exact. This disproves the precise preprint Conjecture D in arXiv:1905.07658v1; final journal wording was inaccessible and a citation discrepancy is disclosed. Sections 9--11 retain the fundamental-gap and separate parameter A, B and C counterexamples in dimensions at least four. No sharp positive-parameter shape optimization theorem or low-dimensional gap result is claimed. This is one internally audited AI-assisted preprint, not independent certification. See [the assessment](papers/P44-robin-corners/ASSESSMENT.md).

## P45: thin reactive-patch spectral measures and uniform response

The claimed strong limit on fixed embedded C3 curves identifies transverse averaging times local width. Every fixed eigenvalue tends to the normalized maximum width; every fixed weight vanishes if the maximum set has zero length. The full input spectral measure and a separate capacity bound give relative response asymptotics uniformly over all positive reactivities. The same paper proves the regular-center-curve versions of Grebenkov-Maurette (B18)-(B19), the one-pole accuracy criterion, and existence of a connected smooth F1>F0 patch. The integral representation, variational principles and approximation are credited prior work. Sharp spectral corrections, positive-length-maximum weight limits, and singular center curves remain outside the theorem. See [the assessment](papers/P45-thin-reactive-patches/ASSESSMENT.md).

## P46: positive radial series and sharp logarithmic concavity

The claimed representation has strictly positive coefficients and convergence radius equal to the squared ball radius. It implies sharp negative-square-root-log concavity and existence of a positive concavity power for every positive solution, in all dimensions. The amplitude threshold, ball uniqueness and shooting order are credited prior results. General convex domains, explicit sharp positive-power exponents and their radius monotonicity are not settled. See [the assessment](papers/P46-logarithmic-ball-concavity/ASSESSMENT.md) for primary-source comparisons and scope limits.

## P47: sharp total variation from spherical rearrangement

The claimed theorem proves V(n,2)=2C(n,2) for every n>=2, with the exact constant and precisely the midpoint point-evaluation extremizers. The proposed advance is a rearrangement inequality for the zero-mean clamped Green form via a positive spherical Poisson mixture. A complementary moment-measure argument proves the p=1 constant identity and coincidence of relaxed maximizers. These results form one paper. The point diagonal, spherical rearrangement and spline identities are credited prior work. Revision 1.1 extends the full identity and midpoint-symmetric extremizer classification to an order-dependent open interval around p=2, using highest-derivative compactness and polynomial sublevel control. The interval is not explicit. The full all-p conjecture, p=1 symmetry, knot classification and ordinary endpoint attainment remain open. See [the assessment](papers/P47-sobolev-variation/ASSESSMENT.md).

## P48: local minimality for the generalized Neumann Hénon problem

The claimed theorem upgrades directional second-variation positivity to strict W1p local minimality modulo scaling below the known limiting Steklov threshold, for large radial-weight exponent. A common weighted space has compact boundary trace; a nonlinear convexity estimate retains concentrated perturbation energy. Above the threshold, the angular test gives instability. The threshold formula, radial convergence and p=2 local-minimum theorem are credited prior work. Revision 1.1 proves a uniform nonlinear neighborhood through the critical trace exponent and matching power bounds on its decay above that exponent, under unit energy normalization and within the positive Steklov range. Equality at the Steklov threshold and the leading radius constant remain unresolved. See [the assessment](papers/P48-henon-local-minima/ASSESSMENT.md).

## P49: sharp annular stability for physical torsion

The claimed theorem gives exponent-two stability for planar physical torsion on a bounded Lipschitz outer domain with finitely many separated Lipschitz holes. The joint-center constant depends only on the ratio of the outer and hole areas; individual asymmetries have a universal constant when B<=2A/3. A second-harmonic perturbation proves optimality. The global calibration theorem of Hensel-Laux and the velocity-deficit strategy of Guerra-Machado-Ramos are credited. The new argument accounts for plateau ranks and propagates a fixed center across an area window. Best constants, endpoint area-ratio limits, nonlinear equations and higher dimensions remain outside the result. See [the assessment](papers/P49-annular-torsion-stability/ASSESSMENT.md).

## P50: all-times metric-graph heat content, killing measures and rigidity

The claimed theorem answers the all-times question in Bifulco-Täufer's final Remark 2.9 for finite compact connected metric graphs with a nonempty Dirichlet set and Kirchhoff conditions elsewhere. There is no arithmetic restriction on edge lengths. A stronger theorem compares cumulative decreasing rearrangements of nonnegative L2 heat evolutions; equality of constant-load heat content at one time characterizes the Dirichlet-Neumann interval. Revision 1.1 extends concentration comparison to finite nonnegative killing measures of fixed total mass on graphs of fixed length, with the endpoint-killed Robin interval as unique maximizer at every time. The atomic torsion consequence is prior work of Özcan-Täufer and is credited. The proof includes form convergence for singular measures and equality at one time. Classical graph energy rearrangement and elliptic-to-parabolic concentration iteration are credited inputs. Revision 1.3 proves the sharp Kohler-Jobin product and fixed-torsion eigenvalue comparisons for arbitrary finite nonnegative killing measures, with equality only for the endpoint-killed interval. A composition test in the weak ground-state equation absorbs the potential energy, and a scalar distribution comparison yields the sharp bound. This settles both general-measure questions left open in revision 1.2. Weighted graphs, signed killing, arbitrary couplings, quantitative stability and heat-trace bounds remain outside the claims. See [the assessment](papers/P50-graph-heat-content/ASSESSMENT.md) for the current source checks, failed search requests and journal-significance limits.
